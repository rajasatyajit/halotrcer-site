## 1. Why

The site is correct, fast and honest, but it reads like documentation. On desktop, every page uses
the left half of a 72 rem column and leaves the right half empty
([www home](before/www-home-desktop-light.jpg)). The hero is text only. The one thing a buyer would
screenshot, the product's output, sits below the fold in a grey terminal or in dense tables
([Sievant sample report](before/sievant-sample-report-desktop-light.jpg)). Nothing on the home
page tells a reader "this is for you", and prices appear once, near the bottom.

Goal: a site that people in the target segments forward to each other, because the thing they
forward (a report, a calculator result, a security review sample) is useful on its own, and
says what it is even when it's cropped.

## 2. Audience (owner's words, condensed)

In order of likelihood to buy. **The order itself never appears on the site.**

1. Seed to Series A AI startups with a fast-growing inference bill (self-hosted vLLM or Ollama,
   or heavy API spend).
2. Small AI agencies and consultancies building LLM apps for clients. Three reasons to buy: a
   report to hand a client, the check resold inside their own work, and a referral arrangement.
3. Startups selling MCP servers or agent products to enterprises. They need an artifact to attach
   to a security questionnaire.
4. Small SaaS companies that recently added AI features. They face a cost spike or a slow
   feature, have small budgets and decide quickly. The free check is their way in.
5. Internal platform or devtools teams at mid-size tech companies.
6. Regulated teams that can't send data to SaaS (healthcare, legal, public sector).
7. Large enterprises.

Not paying, but they matter: individual developers and open-source users (stars, credibility,
referrals). The site isn't designed for them, but they never have to dig for docs, install or
source links.

**Don't target:** payments and fintech. No examples, case studies or copy aimed at that sector.

## 3. Rules every change obeys

All existing gates stay, and a change that breaks one doesn't ship:

- Lighthouse 100 in all four categories (mobile) on key pages; LCP < 1.2 s, CLS 0, INP < 100 ms.
- JS ≤ 20 KB gz per page, page weight ≤ 250 KB. **No new islands are proposed.** Everything
  below is HTML and CSS.
- Strict CSP: no inline scripts or styles, no third-party anything. System fonts only.
- Claim scan: every new sentence that states a product fact, number or quote goes through
  `content/claims.yaml` and `<Claim>`.
- Pre-launch mode: no install commands, versions, GitHub links or star buttons while
  `LAUNCHED=false`.
- WCAG 2.2 AA, both themes, reduced motion, works without JS, and colour is never the only
  signal.
- Every published URL keeps working. This proposal adds pages and removes none, so no redirects
  are needed.

Owner rules added in this round:

- **Labels travel with the artifact.** Anything someone could screenshot or share (a sample
  report section, a table, a calculator result, an OG image) carries "Simulated demo run" or
  "Example" inside its own frame, not only in the text around it.
- **The sole-maintainer question gets a plain answer:** Apache-2.0, a self-contained binary you
  keep, and no account or service to switch off.
- **Prices stay visible.**

## 7. Tooling fixes found during this audit

1. `scripts/build.ts:30` runs `npx astro build`. With an empty `node_modules` this silently
   fetched astro 7.4.1 from the registry. Change it to `npx --no-install astro build`, so a
   missing install fails loudly.
2. CI's `npx playwright install` (ci.yml:156, 196, 234) uses the locked package, but should also
   say `--no-install`.
3. Locally, Playwright 1.64.1 expects Chromium build 1248, and this machine has 1217. Local e2e
   runs will fail until the owner allows a browser download (CLAUDE.md: tell the owner before
   browser downloads). Screenshots for this proposal used the 1217 binary directly. During the
   build, the same binary runs single-page axe checks (both themes) and the after screenshots.
   The full cross-browser e2e run stays in CI (see section 11).
